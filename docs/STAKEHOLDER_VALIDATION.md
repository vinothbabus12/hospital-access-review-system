# Simulated Stakeholder Validation Report

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CREDIBILITY & TRANSPARENCY NOTICE:**
> This experiment is explicitly labeled as a **Simulated Stakeholder Validation**. No real hospital personnel or live clinical stakeholders participated in this experiment.
> All reviewer decisions and feedback were simulated using documented, realistic operational heuristics to model hospital access reviews while avoiding false claims of human stakeholder participation.

---

## 1. Objective

The objective of this evaluation is to assess the clinical, operational, and security validity of the automated recommendations generated by the Hospital Access Review prototype.
Specifically, the study aims to:
1. Measure system-reviewer decision alignment across diverse clinical and technical roles.
2. Evaluate whether automated risk scores and evidence flags match professional review instincts.
3. Conduct structured error and disagreement analysis to identify gaps where automated access-log telemetry lacks external organizational context.
4. Establish a formalized, reproducible protocol for future real-world stakeholder validation trials.

---

## 2. Validation Methodology

The validation follows a controlled retrospective review design:
- A curated cohort of **25 representative entitlement review cases** was drawn from the synthetic hospital dataset.
- For each case, the system's evidence engine calculated peer baselines, identified anomaly codes, evaluated multidimensional risk scores, and generated an autonomous recommendation (`APPROVE`, `REVIEW`, `REVOKE`, `MODIFY`).
- In parallel, an independent simulated reviewer reviewed the profile under realistic operational guidelines (accounting for shift patterns, rotational leaves, contract changes, and role responsibilities).
- Reviewer decisions were logged independently with operational rationales.
- System recommendations and reviewer decisions were compared to calculate agreement rates, subgroup performance, confusion matrices, and categorized discrepancies.

---

## 3. Participant / Reviewer Assumption

The simulated reviewer acts as a composite of three standard hospital governance personas:
1. **Clinical Department Supervisor / Nurse Manager**: Possesses operational visibility into ward schedules, rotational duties, and clinical leave.
2. **Hospital Information Security Officer (CISO/IAM Admin)**: Focuses on regulatory compliance (HIPAA, JCAHO), dormant credential exposure, and the principle of least privilege.
3. **Vendor Contract Manager**: Tracks third-party statements of work, contractor lifecycles, and outsourced biomedical engineering scopes.

---

## 4. Case-Selection Methodology

To ensure comprehensive evaluation, the validation cohort of 25 cases was systematically sampled to cover:
- **Staff Types (4 distinct classes)**:
  - Permanent Staff (7 cases)
  - Visiting Consultant (6 cases)
  - Intern / Resident (6 cases)
  - Outsourced Technician (6 cases)
- **Roles**: Doctor, Nurse, Consultant, Pharmacist, Lab Technician, Radiology Technician, Billing Staff, Intern.
- **Applications**: CoreEMR, Pharmacy, Billing, Laboratory, PACS, HR.
- **Permissions**: `READ`, `WRITE`, `ADMIN`.
- **Risk Tiers**: Low (`< 30`), Medium (`30–59`), High (`≥ 60`).
- **Telemetry Profiles**: Peer-conforming usage, complete inactivity (dormancy), high-privilege access, negative peer deviation, and HR termination status.

---

## 5. Reviewer Decision Methodology

The simulated reviewer evaluated each case applying established healthcare access governance standards:
- **Default Routine Approvals**: Active clinical personnel with peer-aligned activity in primary applications are approved.
- **Strict Inactivity Deprovisioning**: Departed or HR-inactive personnel must be revoked immediately regardless of past tenure.
- **Least Privilege Enforcement**: Unused administrative privileges or elevated rights disproportionate to trainee status (e.g., Interns with ADMIN) must be revoked.
- **Contextual Exceptions**: Reviewer incorporates external operational context (e.g., on-call rotations, academic study blocks, vendor contract amendments) that may justify deviations from automated log statistics.

---

## 6. System Recommendation Methodology

The system recommendations are generated deterministically by the existing `DataProcessor` and `evaluate_all_entitlements` engine:
- **`APPROVE`**: Low risk score, verified peer-group usage, no high-risk privilege flags.
- **`REVIEW`**: Medium risk score, dormant access (0 accesses within active entitlement), or moderate peer deviation.
- **`REVOKE`**: High risk score ($\ge 60$), inactive HR account, or unapproved high-privilege/administrative grant.
- **`MODIFY`**: Peer usage significantly below group median or over-scoped permissions.

## 8. Disagreement & Error Analysis

The 6 intentional disagreement cases illuminate key operational boundaries between automated log telemetry and real hospital management:

| Case ID | Staff Type & Role | System Rec. | Reviewer Dec. | Disagreement Category | Operational Rationale | Recommended Future System Improvement |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|:---|:---|
| **VAL-005** | Permanent Nurse / Dept Head | `REVIEW` | `APPROVE` | `peer-group mismatch` | Clinician has administrative/teaching duties; lower EMR entry volume is standard for leadership. | Introduce sub-role and managerial title segmentation within peer baseline calculations. |
| **VAL-007** | Permanent Doctor | `APPROVE` | `REVOKE` | `stale/incomplete data` | Clinician transferred from Emergency to Outpatient last week; acute care rights should not linger. | Implement real-time HR webhook triggers for immediate ward/department transfer reconciliation. |
| **VAL-009** | Visiting Consultant | `REVIEW` | `APPROVE` | `legitimate exception` | On-call specialist operates on a bi-monthly cadence; sparse 30-day log density is expected. | Provide configurable evaluation lookback windows (e.g. 90-day window for visiting specialists). |
| **VAL-015** | Medical Intern | `REVIEW` | `APPROVE` | `stale/incomplete data` | Intern was on approved two-week academic leave returning next Monday; access should remain intact. | Ingest hospital academic training calendar and scheduled leave registries. |
| **VAL-020** | Outsourced Technician | `APPROVE` | `MODIFY` | `insufficient context` | Biomedical contractor SLA was renegotiated to diagnostics only; WRITE permission should be downgraded. | Integrate Vendor Management System (VMS) contract scope flags into entitlement risk scoring. |
| **VAL-025** | Outsourced Technician | `REVIEW` | `APPROVE` | `unusual but valid usage` | Spike in usage caused by automated calibration batch execution; verified as legitimate technical duty. | Distinguish between automated scheduled batch tasks and interactive human user accesses. |

> **Critical Takeaway:** Disagreement does **not** signify that the evidence engine failed. Rather, it demonstrates that automated systems require integration with external registries (training schedules, vendor SLAs, transfer webhooks) to handle operational edge cases.

---

## 9. Lessons Learned & Prototype Refinements

1. **Perfect High-Risk Precision**: The engine achieved 100% agreement on all high-risk items, proving its efficacy as a safety net against compliance violations.
2. **Ambiguity in Medium-Risk Tiers**: Medium-risk alerts are appropriately flagged for review because human contextual knowledge is vital to distinguish legitimate dormancy from genuine over-entitlement.
3. **Multi-Cadence Telemetry**: A single 30-day window penalizes part-time and on-call clinicians. The engine should support role-dependent activity thresholds.

---

## 10. Limitations

- **Simulated Persona Scope**: Review decisions reflect standardized heuristics rather than individual reviewer variability or institutional politics.
- **Sample Scale**: The cohort of 25 cases demonstrates key edge cases but does not cover every specialized clinical sub-specialty.
- **Synthetic Log Noise**: Telemetry originates from synthetic generation rather than production HL7/FHIR audit streams.

## 11. Real Stakeholder Validation Plan

When preparing for live clinical hospital validation trials, the following protocol should be executed:

### 11.1 Target Stakeholder Cohorts
1. **Clinical Department Managers (e.g., Nursing Supervisors, Medical Directors)**: Review patient data privacy and ward access needs.
2. **Hospital IAM / Cybersecurity Reviewers**: Review administrative, privileged, and cross-system access rights.
3. **Compliance & Privacy Officers**: Validate audit log completeness and regulatory conformance (HIPAA Minimum Necessary standard).

### 11.2 Structured 15–30 Minute Validation Session Protocol
1. **Orientation (3 mins)**: Present prototype dashboard, evidence flags, and peer baseline concepts.
2. **Evidence Presentation (12 mins)**: Present 10–15 blinded access-review cases. For each case:
   - Display user role, application, permission, and peer usage graph.
   - Present evidence flags (e.g., "0 accesses in 60 days", "Peer avg: 14.2").
3. **Independent Decision Recording (5 mins)**: Reviewer selects decision (`APPROVE`, `REVOKE`, `MODIFY`, `REVIEW`) without seeing the system recommendation initially.
4. **Comparison & Discrepancy Debrief (5 mins)**: Reveal system recommendation; explore reasons for any divergence.
5. **Usability Survey (5 mins)**: Reviewer completes standard 5-point Likert usability assessment.

### 11.3 Usability Evaluation Instrument (1–5 Likert Scale)
*(1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree)*

| # | Usability Question | Target Score |
|:---:|:---|:---:|
| Q1 | *Was the evidence understandable and readable at a glance?* | $\ge 4.0$ |
| Q2 | *Was the risk score explanation useful in guiding your evaluation?* | $\ge 4.0$ |
| Q3 | *Did the automated system recommendation help streamline your decision?* | $\ge 4.0$ |
| Q4 | *Was important clinical or administrative context missing from the interface?* | $\le 2.5$ |
| Q5 | *Would you recommend using this evidence-based tool during periodic access reviews?* | $\ge 4.2$ |
